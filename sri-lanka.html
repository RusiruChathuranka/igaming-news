Reported online-gambling share

60–70% of Sri Lankan punters using online/offshore sites has been reported in Sri Lankan media citing authorities. Treat this as a reported estimate, not an official GRA audience measurement. There is no public, authoritative census of unique Sri Lankan online gamblers.

Promotion ecosystem signal

A July 2026 investigation of public Facebook data identified 10,813 records, four betting-branded accounts with about 29,400 followers, 16 groups with about 116,700 members, and 1,000+ WhatsApp invitations. These figures demonstrate promotional reach but must not be interpreted as unique gamblers.

Audience figures are deliberately separated from gambling-user estimates. Overlap, duplicate accounts, ad-reach methodology and age differences mean digital-platform audiences cannot be converted directly into a number of bettors.

Regulatory snapshot

As of 06 Oct 2026
03 Sep 2025

Gambling Regulatory Authority Act, No. 17 of 2025 certified

Parliament established a single statutory framework intended to regulate gambling, including online activity, and replaced the earlier fragmented gambling laws.

01 Dec 2025

Act brought into operation

The Act became operational under Gazette Extraordinary No. 2463/03. Licensing, offences and social-responsibility provisions now form the core framework.

05 Aug 2026

24 unlicensed betting sites blocked

The GRA notified TRCSL and telecom operators were directed to restrict access to 24 identified unlicensed gambling/betting websites. The Finance Ministry warned the public about illegal platforms.

03 Sep 2026

Parliament highlights illegal online gambling enforcement

The Ministerial Consultative Committee on Public Security and Parliamentary Affairs said steps had been taken to block illegal gambling websites and discussed strengthening cybercrime capabilities.

What not to do

High-risk compliance areas

Advertising & acquisition

  • Do not advertise or promote gambling that is being carried on in contravention of the GRA Act.
  • Do not use false, deceptive or misleading claims about gambling, risks or chances of winning.
  • Do not market gambling as a solution to financial problems or as a route to improved social status.
  • Do not target, accept or facilitate gambling by anyone under 18.
  • Do not assume an overseas licence automatically authorises gambling activity in Sri Lanka.
  • Do not use government logos, official-looking language or government endorsements to create false legitimacy.
  • Do not use affiliates, influencers, WhatsApp groups or surrogate pages as a workaround for restrictions on an unlicensed operator.

Operational compliance

  • Verify the Sri Lankan licensing position before launching Sri Lanka-facing gambling activity.
  • Maintain robust 18+ controls and prevent underage access.
  • Maintain responsible-gambling controls and clear risk information.
  • Assess AML and illicit-finance risks and maintain appropriate controls.
  • Keep advertising, affiliate and sponsorship records so campaigns can be audited.
  • Separate editorial reporting from paid gambling promotion and clearly identify commercial relationships.
  • Monitor regulator, TRCSL and Finance Ministry notices because enforcement can change quickly.
Important legal noteThis page is an industry-research reference, not legal advice. Licensing conditions, regulations, tax treatment and advertising requirements can change. For a live commercial decision, confirm the current position with the GRA/Ministry of Finance and qualified Sri Lankan legal counsel.

Key national bodies

Who matters in the Sri Lankan ecosystem?
GRA
Gambling Regulatory Authority
Primary gambling regulator: licensing, supervision, enforcement, social-responsibility framework and action against unlawful gambling.
TRCSL
Telecommunications Regulatory Commission
Telecom/network regulator; in 2026 it directed operators to restrict access to identified unlicensed gambling websites following GRA notification.
IRD
Inland Revenue Department
Administers betting and gaming levy/tax obligations. IRD's 2026 tax calendar lists an 18% gross-collection payment for betting & gaming.
FIU / CBSL
Financial Intelligence Unit
Relevant to AML, suspicious transactions and illicit-finance risk. The GRA Act expressly requires gambling risk assessment around money laundering and illicit financial activity.

Other relevant institutions

Sri Lanka Police — investigations and enforcement; Ministry of Finance, Planning and Economic Development — policy and GRA oversight; Data Protection Authority — personal-data compliance where customer/marketing data is processed; Attorney General's Department — legal/prosecution framework.

Tax signal

IRD's current online information lists betting and gaming obligations, while its 2026 tax calendar specifies 18% of turnover/gross collection for the relevant betting & gaming payment. Tax and licensing are separate questions: paying a levy does not itself make an unlicensed operation lawful.
